# Characters in your world

A cat can notice someone opening a cupboard, approach its food, and settle onto a couch. You can
author those responses with ordinary objects, messages, and reactions.

An **actor** is an object that represents a character. It has abilities such as speaking and
receiving gifts. Players use actor abilities, and you can create actors that are not controlled by a
connected person. These characters are often called **NPCs**, short for non-player characters.

[Make room for Henri](henri-and-the-cupboard.md) builds a cat and the objects around him. The lesson
introduces his mood, responses to petting and food, and recurring activity. It follows
[the pet rock](../programming/pet-rock.md), so start there if verbs and reactions are new to you.

## Describing a character

A name and pronouns help messages refer to the character. Activities explain what the character is
doing. Reactions connect events, such as a cupboard opening, to responses you have chosen.

The [names lesson](../building/names-and-grammar.md) and
[message guide](../behaviour/pronouns-and-messages.md) cover those parts of the description. The
book still needs a separate guide to activities, timers, and schedules, including stopping them and
understanding what survives a restart.

## Characters that use language models

Henri's tutorial uses authored responses. It does not need a language model or a model provider.
Cowbell also includes characters and tools that do use language models, such as Mr. Welcome. Those
have additional configuration and operating requirements. They have their
[own section](../language-models/index.md).
