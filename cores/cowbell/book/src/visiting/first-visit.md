# Your first visit

You need the address of a Cowbell world and an account there. Its operator can tell you how to
connect. If you want to start your own world, see [running a world](../running/index.md).

After you connect, you arrive in a room. Its description tells you where you are, what is nearby,
and where you can go. Other people in that room can see your speech and actions.

Try `look` to read the room again. Use `help` to find command help, including `help say` and
`help emote` for conversation. `exits` lists ways out of the room.

In a fresh Cowbell world, type `examples` from the First Room to visit the Travel Gallery. The rooms
there explain ordinary passages, doors and keys, one-way travel, and portals. Type `out` from the
gallery to return to the First Room.

## Meadow and terminal clients

Meadow shows the conversation alongside controls for inspecting and editing the world. Room and
object links can offer actions. A terminal client presents the world as text, with commands entered
at its prompt.

Both clients connect to the same world. The objects, their owners, and their permissions are the
same whichever client you use. The tutorials give commands you can type, even when Meadow also
offers a button or an editor for that task.

A full tour of Meadow and its terminal equivalents is still to be written. The
[Meadow project page](https://github.com/timbran-project/moor/blob/main/clients/meadow/README.md)
describes its current interface and editing tools.

## From visiting to making

You can build rooms and objects when your account has builder privileges. Programmer privileges also
let you write code. These are separate permissions: you can make a responsive cupboard without
writing a program for it.

When you are ready, continue with [start building](../building/index.md). If you want to learn
programming through a small project, try [the pet rock](../programming/pet-rock.md).
